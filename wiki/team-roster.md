## Roles Are Sunglasses, Not Job Titles

**No one is locked into a single job.** Across the season every student tries all four ways of looking at a problem — 🛠️ Operator sees tools and moving parts, ⚙️ Technician sees sensors and data, 🌱 Specialist sees how living things fit together, 🚁 Driver sees the big picture from above. Sharing those views is the foundation of the team's teamwork. Full framing: [[project-paths]].

**On match day it tightens up:** the rulebook requires one player per role for that match, and no switching once it starts. So the season's job is to make sure every student has worn every pair of glasses, and that each match has someone confident in each seat.

## The Four Match Roles

Four students take the field per match, one per role. Each stays in their own area for the whole match, each has one specific tool, and **nothing may be handed between players**. Any player may attempt any mission. (source: fll-future-3-8-bioglow-rulebook.pdf)

| Role | Tool | Suits a student who… |
|------|------|----------------------|
| **Driver** | Controller + driving base (double motor). May never touch the base by hand. | has steady hands, stays calm on the clock |
| **Operator** | Mechanical tool, non-electric LEGO only. Tool never leaves their area. | likes building mechanisms and precise manual work |
| **Technician** | Robotic tool: single motor + color sensor. May service the driving base. | enjoys tinkering and quick fixes mid-match |
| **Specialist** | Controller + grand tree double motor + laptop running team programs. **Can operate other players' tools.** | is the strongest programmer — heaviest coding load |

Full constraints and courtesy rules: [[bioglow-missions]].

## Five Students, Four Roles

One student sits out each match. That is not a problem to solve — it is a structural advantage, but only if the rotation is planned:

- Events run **at least three matches**, and rank is the **average** across them, so a weak substitute match drags the whole score. Every role needs two students who can actually play it.
- The rotation therefore *forces* cross-training. Treat "backup" as a real assignment, not a label.
- Rotating everyone through matches also serves Inclusion in [[core-values]] — nobody should spend the whole tournament on the sideline.

**Practical target**: each student learns **one primary and one backup role**, so all four roles have two trained players across five kids.

**25 October:** the roles were **rotated across eight full scored rounds**, so each kid played different seats under the clock. The coach's main takeaway was that **each role needs a readiness check before the run starts** — many of the lost points were setup issues, not driving or design. See [[season-journal]].

Three official matches, four seats, five students. (source: coach, revised 1 November 2026)

| | **G1** | **G2** | **G3** |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Specialist** | Cheryl | Kyle | Cheryl |
| **Driver** | Kyle | Kei | **Lola** |
| **Operator** | Kei | Lindsey | **Kyle** |
| **Technician** | Lola | Cheryl | Lindsey |
| **Sits out** | *Lindsey* | *Lola* | *Kei* |

**What the grid gets right:**

- **Every student sits out exactly once.** Nobody spends the tournament on the sideline — Inclusion in [[core-values]], and it holds by construction rather than by goodwill on the day.
- **The Specialist seat is owned.** Cheryl takes it in G1 and G3, Kyle in G2. That role runs the grand tree, which is what lifts M02 resources from 5 points to 10 — see [[bioglow-missions]]. The ⚠️ gap carried on this page since Week 4 is closed.
- **Driver is now three deep.** The 1 November revision put **Lola** in the G3 Driver seat and moved Kyle to Operator. That single swap took Driver from two trained players to three and is the main change from the first draft.
- **Kyle plays three different roles in three matches** — Driver, Specialist, Operator. The most versatile seat on the team, and worth saying out loud to judges.

**One seat is still only two players deep:**

| Role | Students who play it | Depth |
|------|---------------------|------:|
| **Specialist** | Cheryl, Kyle | ⚠️ **2** |
| **Driver** | Kyle, Kei, Lola | 3 |
| **Operator** | Kei, Lindsey, Kyle | 3 |
| **Technician** | Lola, Cheryl, Lindsey | 3 |

**Neither Lindsey nor Lola ever plays Specialist**, and Lindsey never plays Driver. If Cheryl or Kyle wakes up ill on event morning, the Specialist seat has nobody trained — and because game rank is the **average** across all matches ([[judging-and-awards]]), a badly staffed match cannot be dropped. Specialist is also the worst seat to improvise in: controller, grand tree double motor and the laptop running the team's programs.

**Cheryl and Kyle play all three matches**, with no rest. Fine across 2:30 a match; worth watching over a full tournament day that also holds a 24-minute judging session.

**Why this matters more than it looks.** The eight scored rounds on 25 October ran **135, 65, 95, 165, 125, 5, 125, 185** — a mean of about **112** with a range from **5 to 185**. The spread, not the ceiling, is what the average-of-three rule punishes, and the coach's own takeaway was that most lost points were **setup issues, not driving or design**. A seat played by someone who has never sat in it is exactly how a 5 happens.

**Fix before January:** one practice run at **Specialist** for Lindsey and for Lola, plus one at **Driver** for Lindsey. Week 16 (Sun 15 Nov) is set aside for it — see [[calendar]].

## Team Agreements

**Parents**
- Text the group in advance if a child will miss a session — pair and group work depends on attendance, and with five students an absence changes the role rotation
- "Embrace the mess" — step back and let the kids figure it out

**Students**
1. Share the fun and the work — the robot and computers belong to the whole team
2. Listen to understand — "tell me more about that idea," not "that won't work"
3. Respect space and teammates — talk about the idea, not the person
4. Celebrate mistakes — ask "what did we learn?" and try a new way

See [[gracious-professionalism]] and [[core-values]].
