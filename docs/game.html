Field notebook

All of this is true

  • Lacewing eggs sit on silk stalks — the mother lifts each egg off the leaf so the first larva to hatch cannot immediately eat the others.
  • Larvae are cannibals. Insectaries rear them one to a cell for exactly this reason.
  • They do not eat plants. A lacewing larva is a predator. Lettuce in the cup is an empty cup.
  • Never spray water in. A droplet drowns a newly hatched larva. Moisture comes from its food.
  • They spin a cocoon on folded paper. No hiding place, no pupation.
  • Ants farm aphids for honeydew and drive off the insects that come to eat them.
  • Adults eat nectar, not pests. No flowers and they leave to find some.
  • Broad-spectrum spray kills everything, and the pests return first, because they breed faster.
