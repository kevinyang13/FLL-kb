Field notebook

All of this is true

  • Lacewing eggs sit on silk stalks — the mother lifts each egg off the leaf so the first larva to hatch cannot immediately eat the others.
  • Larvae are cannibals. Insectaries rear them in separate cells for exactly this reason. Put two in one cup and one of them comes out.
  • They do not eat plants. A lacewing larva is a predator. Lettuce in the cup is an empty cup.
  • Never spray water in. A droplet drowns a newly hatched larva. Moisture comes from the food, or a damp cotton ball.
  • They spin a cocoon on folded paper. Give them somewhere hidden or they cannot pupate.
  • Ants farm aphids for their honeydew and will drive off or eat the beneficial insects that come to help. A garden with an ant trail stays a garden with aphids.
  • Adults eat nectar and pollen, not pests. No flowers, and they leave to find some.
  • Broad-spectrum spray kills everything, and the pests come back first, because they breed faster.
