Project Paths

Summary: Future Edition splits the research work into four role-specific Project Paths. They are the official research scaffold, they feed both tool design and the project presentation, and the Project rubric scores them by name.

Sources: raw/bioglow/fll-future-3-8-path-{driver,operator,technician,specialist}.pdf, fll-future-3-8-judging-rubric.pdf

Last updated: 2026-08-08

Four Pairs Of Magic Sunglasses

Nobody is locked into one job. The biggest change in FLL this year is learning to observe the world differently, and the four roles are best understood as four pairs of magic sunglasses. Everyone tries on every pair.

Glasses What you see when you wear them
🛠️ Operator Physical tools and moving parts
⚙️ Technician Sensors, data, and coding ideas
🌱 Specialist How plants, bugs and nature work together
🚁 Driver The big picture, from the sky

Take turns wearing them and you learn to see a problem from several angles at once. Sharing those different views with each other is the real foundation of teamwork — and it is what the Core Values rows are looking for.

How this squares with match day

The two are not in conflict, and it is worth being clear with the kids:

Roles work like…
Learning, field trips, thinking Sunglasses. Everyone tries all four, all season.
During a match One player per role, fixed for that match — the rulebook requires it, and nobody may switch once the match begins

So: wear every pair while you are learning; pick one to wear for each match. Rotating across matches means everyone still gets a turn — see team roster.

What They Are

Each of the four roles also gets its own Project Path — a short research brief on how real scientists do that role's job in a rainforest. Think of the path as the reading that goes with each pair of glasses. Students research their path, then use what they learn twice:

  1. To design their own role's tool
  2. To feed the team's project work for judging

This is a real structural difference from the classic format, where one team picks one topic. Here the research is distributed across four students along the same lines as the match roles. See team roster.

The Four Paths

Role Research topic Brief Vocabulary
Driver Drones & aerial technology "Move through the rainforest and finish missions. Learn how aerial technology like drones is used around the world. Use what you learn to design your Driver tool." survey, drone, sensor, navigate, aerial technology
Operator Ecologists' mechanical tools "Design a mechanical tool that can deliver resources to the grand tree. See how ecologists use mechanical tools in real life." nets, forceps, ecology, species, reforestation
Technician Biologists monitoring animals "Design a robotic tool with a sensor and a motor to help the rainforest. See how biologists use tools to monitor animals." balance, motion-activated camera, biology, sound recorders, endangered
Specialist Foresters & preservation "Operate the grand tree and cycle resources. See how foresters work to preserve places like the rainforest. Use what you learn to help your game strategy." GPS, mapping technology, forester, sensor, tree corer

Each path is structured the same way: a background reading, then guiding questions grouped under People & Careers, Technology, and Impact.

Students are told to research further using "trusted educational websites, books, and news articles written for students, videos of expert interviews, and tools provided by your teacher or coach." That last clause is the opening for the expert interviews in innovation project — a biologist pairs naturally with the Technician path, a forester or ecologist with the Specialist and Operator paths.

Why This Matters for Scoring

The Project rubric's first section is literally titled "EXPLORE: How does the team research the project path?" and scores two rows:

  • Team clearly explains how the guided research influenced their project direction
  • Team uses multiple relevant sources to learn about their selected prompt

A team that runs a generic brainstorm and never touches the Project Paths cannot score well on those rows — the rubric asks about the path by name. See judging and awards.

The connection also runs the other way. The Engineering Design rubric rewards "custom mechanical builds" and "custom coding that supports their mission strategy" — and each path explicitly ends with "use what you learn to design your tool." Path research is meant to be visible in the hardware.

How To Run Them

  • Assign paths with roles. A student's path matches their match role, so path research and tool building reinforce each other. Assign both together by Week 5 — see calendar.
  • Do not silo it. Every student presents their path findings to the team; the project presentation is one team story, not four.
  • Capture sources. "Multiple relevant sources" is a scored row. Log every source in the Engineering Notebook as you go, not the week before the event.
  • Aim interviews at the paths. One expert can serve two or three paths at once.
  • On a site visit, give each student their own questions. A printable role sheet turns one group walk into four parallel investigations — per-site detail in innovation project ideas.
