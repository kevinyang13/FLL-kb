2026-10-16 — A hand lens

Z steps the lens in, X steps it out, the mouse wheel does both, and there is a Zoom button on touch. Three steps beyond the crouch view — 3.4×, 6.2×, 11.3× — quoted against the walking view so the number means what a child expects.

Three things it needed beyond changing the field of view:

  • Its own light. Up against a leaf the camera sits inside the shrub's own shadow, and a magnified dark green blob teaches nothing. A small point light rides the camera, off at walking magnification.
  • A narrower selection cone. The crosshair covers a far smaller slice of the world through a lens, so the cone scales with the field of view — otherwise a zoomed view still picks things well outside the frame.
  • A steadier head. The walk bob is magnified along with everything else; it drops to 15% while the lens is up.

2026-10-16 — "Can't find anything": four reasons, all fixed

Play-tested by script — a robot that only knows what the player knows: face the compass, walk, crouch, press E. It found 4 of 13. Four separate causes:

  1. All nine scenes landed in one corner. Anchors were resolved by list[n % length], and the perch list is generated in build order, so every scene took a perch from the same few metres of border. Scenes are now spread along the garden's length, alternating sides, so walking the path takes you past all of them. They now range from 2 m to 31 m from the spawn.
  2. Half of them were buried inside the shrub banks, three metres deep in foliage where a player cannot stand or see. Only the lawn-facing face of a front-row clump offers a perch now.
  3. Already-found things swallowed every look. The twenty-six aphids on the colony leaf outranked the mummy and the two larvae sitting among them, so the scene read as "already found" forever and the robot stalled there repeatedly. Anything already written up now steps aside in the targeting score.
  4. Nothing marked a scene. A constant-size glowing mote now floats over each unfinished scene, and a compass gives bearing and distance to the nearest one.

The same robot now finds 11 of 13 by walking. The two it misses — the cocoon on a trunk and the spider in the hedge — were each verified reachable from all six approach directions; the robot simply cannot path around a tree.

Also: the targeting cone widens as you close in (18° at range, 55° at arm's length). Demanding precise aim at a two-millimetre insect you are standing on top of was most of the rest of the problem.

2026-10-15 — Audit: everything in the garden is on the ground

Four separate faults, all found by raycasting the terrain under every instance rather than by eye:

  1. The terrain was mirrored. A PlaneGeometry rotated -90° about X maps its local +Y to world -Z, so displacing each vertex by H(x, localY) built the ground back to front against everything standing on it — every plant, and the player's own feet, used H(x, z) while the ground beneath them was H(x, -z).
  2. A hundred forest trees stood over nothing. The ring of trees reaches 46 m out; the ground stopped at 32 m. The land is now drawn to 170 m while the player is still fenced into the 64 m garden.
  3. The ridge was buried, summit 20 m below the lawn — never visible in any frame. Replaced with two ridges behind the garden.
  4. Two thirds of tree crowns touched nothing. Crown blobs were scattered above the trunk; they are now grown outward from one central mass sitting on the trunk top, and trunk thickness scales with the crown it holds up.

Verified by measurement, not by looking: 0 instances with no ground beneath them (was 94), 0 unsupported shrub blobs, 0 disconnected crown blobs (was 67), 0 floating bedding cushions.

A fifth followed: the whole forest leaned over. instanced() reuses one THREE.Object3D to compose each matrix, and the forest placer set a position and a scale but no rotation — so every cone silently inherited the random tilt of the last shrub blob placed. The shared object is now reset before each placement, which is the kind of bug that only a shared mutable scratch object produces. Worst tilt off vertical is now 0° for every trunk, conifer and forest tree.

2026-10-12 — Lacewing Garden, a browser game

Created: docs/game.html — a single-page game. Raise lacewings from egg to adult on an indoor bench, release them into a backyard garden, and get the aphids under control. Linked from the homepage, outreach event, lacewing project and urls.

Every failure in it is a real one, and each prints the reason in a field notebook rather than just ending the run: two larvae in a cup and one comes out; a sprayed droplet drowns a young larva; lettuce goes untouched because larvae are predators; no folded paper means nowhere to pupate; above 90°F kills, below 60°F stalls.

Two mechanics carry the ideas that are hardest to say in a sentence.

Aphids are both the pest and the food. Collecting them from the garden to feed the bench costs you garden progress, which is the awkward truth of rearing predators and exactly the problem the real team hit in September.

Ants guard the aphids. From Tracy's interview — they farm them for honeydew and drive off the beneficials, so the garden can hold plenty of predators and still lose. There is also a pesticide button that works, once, and takes every beneficial insect with it.

The win condition is not zero aphids. It is aphids held low with three kinds of beneficial insect living there, which is what control actually means — a garden with no pests has nothing to feed the predators and they leave. The end card says so.

Visual direction: a garden at dusk, warm lamplight on the bench indoors against cool twilight outside, with the lacewing's pale green as the accent — a nod to BIOGLOW without making it a cartoon. Critters are hand-drawn SVG, including the egg on its silk stalk.

Also published as a shareable artifact. The repo copy is a standalone document with its own charset and viewport, since the artifact platform supplies those and a web server does not.

Reported: after clicking the view, the player keeps walking and cannot be stopped.

Cause: a dropped keyup. Taking or releasing the mouse puts a browser notification bar on screen, and the keyup for a key held at that moment is delivered to the bar rather than to the page. 2026-10-20 — Lacewing Garden v2: auditing the river's edges

Asked for: audit the river edges and make sure the water is not floating.

It was, by about a metre, and the earlier audits had missed it because they measured the analytic height field — which was and still is exact — rather than the terrain mesh that is actually drawn. Raycasting the rendered mesh along the whole river found two faults:

before after
River: dry ground under the water line, just outside the ribbon 0.95 m 0.25 m
River: terrain poking up through the water 0.17 m 0.19 m
Lake: terrain poking up through the water 1.16 m 0.08 m
Lake: dry ground under the water line 0.09 m 0.16 m

Two causes, both real:

  1. The bed met the water vertically. Every bed was a half ellipse, so its edge is a cliff. The mesh samples every four metres and cannot place that edge, so the chord between a vertex in the channel and one on the bank crossed the waterline wherever it liked. Raising the bank first only traded the fault for its opposite — terrain poking 0.94 m up through the water instead. The beds are now a straight V: full depth in the middle, meeting the surface at the rim at a steady 1 in 4, which four-metre sampling follows easily and which is also what a river's cross-section looks like.
  2. The lake castle's terrace was swallowing the shoreline. levelLakeTerrace flattens the ground around the palace to its floor, and its blend reached to within eight metres of the lake's rim — standing the shore up as a two-metre bank right at the water, which the mesh then smeared into the lake. The terrace now fades out over the last twelve metres before the water. The palace's own floor is still dead level at −1.00 across its whole footprint.

Verified after: 0 plants standing in water, 0 analytically flooded spots with no water drawn, all four mesh faults at or under 25 cm, 61 fps, no console errors. Looked at the bank from eye level: the water meets the grass on both sides with no gap and no hanging edge.

Worth keeping in mind: an analytic height field being exact says nothing about the mesh built from it. Any future shoreline work should be checked by raycasting what is drawn.
