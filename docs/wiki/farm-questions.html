20 Questions to Ask at Coastal Roots Farm

Summary: A question bank for the 16 August visit — five sets of four, so every student can carry their own. Marked with the five that matter most. Printable version.

Sources: team prep; biodiversity at the farm

Last updated: 2026-08-14

The kids ask these, not the adults. That is the FLL rule and it is also a scored rubric row — judges ask what the team learned from experts and what they changed because of it. An adult asking a great question produces nothing for the notebook. See gracious professionalism.

Five students, twenty questions — four each. Let them pick the ones they actually want answered rather than assigning them.

⭐ The Five That Matter Most

If the tour is short or the kids get shy, these five are the ones to protect:

# Question Why it matters
6 "What happens to our food scraps after they go in the compost, and how long does it take?" Compost is M01 Mighty Microbiomes in the real world
9 "How do you decide when the plants need water?" Leads straight to smart irrigation — our best prototype idea
13 "Which job here is still done by hand, and why hasn't a machine taken over?" Finds a real problem worth solving
16 "Is there a job here you wish a machine could do for you?" This is asking the expert to hand you a project. Best single question on the list
20 "What would happen to this land if the farm stopped taking care of it?" The "if nobody does anything" answer the Project rubric wants

Set 1 · Warm-up — anyone can ask these

Good openers. They get the guide talking and settle nervous kids.

  1. What is your favourite thing about working here?
  2. How many different kinds of plants and animals live on the farm?
  3. What lives here that most visitors never notice?
  4. What is the hardest part of your job?

Set 2 · 🌱 Specialist glasses — how living things fit together

  1. How do the trees, the vegetables and the chickens help each other in the food forest?
  2. ⭐ What happens to our food scraps after they go in the compost, and how long does it take?
  3. How can you tell if the soil is healthy or sick?
  4. Which animals do you want on the farm, and which ones are a problem?

Set 3 · ⚙️ Technician glasses — sensors, measuring, data

  1. ⭐ How do you decide when the plants need water?
  2. Does anything here measure something by itself, without a person checking?
  3. How do you use less water than a normal farm?
  4. What do you write down or keep track of every day?

Set 4 · 🛠️ Operator glasses — tools and moving parts

  1. ⭐ Which job here is still done by hand, and why hasn't a machine taken over?
  2. What tool do you use the most?
  3. How do you deal with weeds and pests without spraying poison?
  4. ⭐ Is there a job here you wish a machine could do for you?

Set 5 · 🚁 Driver glasses — the big picture

  1. If you could look down at the whole farm from the sky, what would you check for?
  2. Which parts of the farm do you leave alone on purpose, and why?
  3. How is the farm different in summer than in winter?
  4. ⭐ What would happen to this land if the farm stopped taking care of it?

How To Ask Well

The best question is the second one. After any answer, one of these gets you the real material:

  • "Why?"
  • "Can you show me?"
  • "How do you know?"
  • "What happens if that goes wrong?"

A tour guide answering "we compost the scraps" is polite. A tour guide walking you over to the pile because a kid asked "can you show me?" is a project.

Three habits worth naming before you go:

  1. Ask one question, then stop talking. Silence is where the good answer arrives.
  2. Write the answer down straight away — on the Farm Visit Sheet or any paper. Nobody remembers by evening.
  3. Say thank you and use their name. Judges do ask how the team treated the people who helped.

What To Do With The Answers

Same day, into the Drive:

What Where
Notes and answers 04 Innovation Project/Expert Interviews/
Photos of anything you asked about 07 Media/
The farm as a source 04 Innovation Project/Sources Log/
Anything that changed your thinking The workbook — "what we changed because of it"

That last row is the one the rubric scores. See weekly documentation and judging and awards.
