Sun 9 AugWeek 2

Most of the field is built

The bulk of the mission models went together: grand tree, hive, nest, cave waterfall, token recycler and operator barrier. A few structures went home between families to finish during the week — which means the field only exists when everyone turns up with their part.

The team’s own entry says who built what: Kyle the grand tree, hive and operator barrier; Lindsey the nest, grand tree, cave waterfall and barrier; Cheryl the grand tree, token recycler and barrier; Lola the grand tree and recycler; Kei the grand tree and cave waterfall. Patience while building was recorded as a Core Values moment: Kei and Kyle on the hive, Cheryl and Lola, Lindsey and Kei.

Sun 16 AugWeek 3

The field is finished and the kids play the game

After the morning at the farm, the rest of the day was build and practice. All mission structures were complete, all motors installed, and the first Driver robot and Technician tool were built with basic coding. The Operator tool was finished too, ahead of the week’s goal.

The team also learned how the field is loaded with keystone species and resources, then played five familiarisation run-throughs at the full 2:30, with everyone rotating through every role. They were not scored matches. They were the first time the kids saw the game as a game, and a chance to feel how long 2:30 is.

Sun 30 AugWeek 5

A collector on the base, a ramp on the Technician

The Week 4 goals landed. The driving base now ran with a collector, the Technician’s launcher was replaced by a ramp, and the Operator’s tools were improved for grand-tree accuracy. Sun 6 SepWeek 6
Scores & runsRobot buildsRules & strategySource: Journal + photos

First points on the board: 100

The first real score: about 100 points across 10-plus runs. The driving base was rebuilt strong and navigated to the young forest by program; the Technician ramp delivered three keystone species into the microbiome. That is 60 points on mission M01 alone, with the rest from elsewhere on the field.

The team also made its first strategy call: split the grand tree in two so one structure serves both the cave waterfall and the young forest. It is a refusal to pick between parking tokens (20 points once) and cycling them (5 points a time): build a dispenser that feeds both.

A home run worth 210

The next day a home run, filmed by Kevin and tallied by Jason, scored 210: three keystone species in each of two microbiomes (120), the M01 bonus for knocking down the invasive queen (40), and M04 — all species out of the nest (30) and all resources out of the hollow tree (20).

Kevin’s note that day is the honest one: “the base is definitely a bit too big so it doesn’t have much room for error.” That is the decision to shrink it, made two days before the meeting.

Sun 13 SepWeek 7
Robot buildsRules & strategySource: Journal + photos

Three decisions: small, a dispenser, program first

1 — The base is small, simple and sticky. Lighter means it stops where it was told; grippy wheels mean a counted rotation is a rotation the robot made. 2 — The dispenser replaces the ramp: it carries six keystone species and releases three at a time, which is exactly the most a microbiome scores, so one release fills one microbiome for 60 points. That is the third Technician design (launcher, ramp, dispenser), the evidence the Engineering Design rubric wants. 3 — Program first, controller to correct: runs vary slightly, so the program drives and the Driver nudges the last few millimetres.

Three projects, run side by side

After the morning farm tour the afternoon was robots, now organised as three named projects. Project 1 (Cheryl and Lola) — young forest, nest and hollow tree, missions M01 and M04: the software improved, but drift correction on the controller was still unreliable. Project 2 (Kyle, Lindsey and Kei) — cave waterfall: the tower lifts a keystone and rotates, and the grabber that feeds a token into it was not designed yet. Project 3 (Kei) — the Operator tool: an improved tool was decided.

Two candidate feeders were sketched: a hand-pressed bell crank, and a tread-triggered version seen in another team’s video. It was also the “40% week”: any mission scoring under 40% of attempts gets cut.

Sun 4 OctWeek 10
Robot buildsRules & strategySource: Journal + photos

Three bases, one jig — and meshers

Each kit family now had an identical base, which let Projects 1 and 2 run at the same time. The design carries both a double and a single motor and swaps two attachments by gears. Kei finished the pickup tool, with a rubber band to help the grab.

The decision that matters most: the team learned to build meshers, parts shaped to fit into the hollow tree, cave waterfall and nest, so the tool locks onto the model itself. The reason the kids gave was to avoid inconsistency between the software and the real table. With no gyro, the robot only has to get close; the geometry does the rest.

Sun 1 NovWeek 14
Scores & runsRobot buildsRoles & teamRules & strategySource: Journal + photos

The spread closed — and the base failed its height check

Four scored runs: 170, 140, 105, 160. Average 143.75 against 112.5 a week earlier, and the lowest run rose from 5 to 105. Game rank is the average across at least three matches, so the floor matters more than the ceiling. The coach’s clearest positive: Specialist, Operator, Driver and Technician were talking to each other during runs.

The problems were all on the base. It is too low to deliver into the hollow tree and, as relayed, over the 8-inch inspection height, so the obvious fix makes the legal problem worse. The decision was to stop raising the robot: the grand tree drops into the hollow tree instead, and the driving base no longer travels there. The cave waterfall has a stronger new ramp that is still big and tall.

The kids also wrote down their top two roles. The whiteboard photographed at 1:52 pm is the resulting grid: Operator Kyle, Kei, Lindsey; Driver Kei, Lola; Specialist Cheryl, Kyle; Technician Lola, Lindsey, Cheryl. The earlier photo shows the picks circled, with the four scores in purple.

Measured that evening, loosely. At 8:41 pm someone held a tape measure against the base: about 7½ to 8 inches at the top of the frame, but at an angle, and no number was written down. That frame looks like the rebuilt one (see 2 Nov), so the rebuild was already under way.
