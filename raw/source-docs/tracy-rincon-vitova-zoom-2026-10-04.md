# Zoom interview — Tracy, Rincon-Vitova Insectaries
Date: 2026-10-04 (notes as supplied by team, lightly annotated by note-taker in Chinese)

## 1. How quickly will we see fewer pests?
Q: "How soon do they usually see fewer pests?"
A: "It depends on what pest it is, how many there are, and how many lacewings you put out. One problem is ants. Ants protect the pests because they like the honeydew. They'll fight off the beneficial insects and eat them."

## 2. How do you put lacewings into containers?
Q: "What tools do you use to get the lacewings inside containers?"
A: "If they're adults or juveniles, we catch them with a little net and put them in there. But usually, we start with eggs. That way, we can put them wherever we need them.
"We put a bunch of eggs in each container, and one will be left over. I would put a few eggs in each container and let the strongest one survive. That's what they do in the wild. Separating them is difficult, especially when you're mass-producing."

## 3. What does a lacewing condo need?
Q: "We are making a lacewing condo for home gardeners. What are the three most important things?"
A: "Food and shelter. When they pupate and make their cocoon, they like places where they can hide.
"Get a little strip of paper, fold it like an accordion, and put it in with them. They'll make their cocoon on that little piece of paper. We do that, too."
[Note-taker: she named only two — food and shelter.]

## 4. How do we give them water safely?
Q: "Larvae can drown in drops of water. How do you give them water safely?"
A: "If they have enough food, they get their moisture from the food. For the adults, we put a little sponge with water in with them, and they drink off the sponge."

## 5. What ingredients do you use in their food?
Q: "What ingredients do you use to make their food?"
A: "It depends on the diet and the life stage. I think they feed the adults honey and yeast. The juvenile babies eat moth eggs.
"Another insectary we know uses a hamburger and egg diet. The problem with that diet is that it goes bad pretty quickly, and it's kind of gross."
[Note-taker: she said "I think" about the adult food — not fully confirmed.]

## 6. Which of our three foods would help them grow better?
Q: "We are testing three foods: moth eggs, fruit flies that cannot fly, and aphids from a local farm. Which would help lacewings grow better?"
A: "They eat all of those naturally in the wild, but fresh food is best. I would say aphids and fruit flies."

## 7. Should we use a wet sponge for our lacewings?
Q: "We also raise ladybugs, and they keep a sponge inside. They tell us to water it every other day. Should we do that for our lacewings?"
A: "I don't know. That would be good to find out. If you do, make sure they don't drown in it. That would be a good experiment for you guys because I'm not really sure."

## 8. What are lacewings scared of?
Q: "Is there anything lacewings are scared of?"
A: "Big, giant people and big, giant children looking at them? Maybe they are. That's why they want to hide.
"They're little, and anything bigger than them might eat them. Sometimes they're afraid, and sometimes they're not. They're pretty voracious. Maybe ants — ants can eat them."

## Parent follow-up: Where do they make their cocoons?
Q: "Do they usually make their cocoons at the top or on the leaves?"
A: "They like protected areas where they have cover. I believe they also make cocoons in leaf litter on the ground.
"With a little folded piece of paper, they'll find a protected place in there and make their cocoon."
